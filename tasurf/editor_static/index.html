Getting started

  1. Game & folders. TASurf finds Counter-Strike: Source in your Steam libraries and SVR in the usual places; pick them with Browse… only when it can't. Pick a map and press Launch: the game starts through SVR in a window behind TASurf. Join a team: you fly in edit mode (noclip, no weapons), and the replay bot TAS joins your team. It plays everything the editor sends, so you can watch from anywhere.
  2. Capture a start. Fly to where the run should begin and type tas_setstart name in the game's console. Or record yourself playing: tas_mode record, then tas_record to start and again to save (bind it to a key).
  3. Create a timeline (Create timeline): from that start position, from your recording, or as a copy.
  4. Edit it on the Edit page (the game previews every edit), and Export it to video.

Folders and settings

The project folder holds your timelines (its timelines folder) and exported videos (videos). With "Keep SourceMod off", Metamod's loader is renamed while no TASurf session runs, so a game you start from Steam is stock; if a session ends without TASurf (it was closed first, or crashed), it goes off again the next time TASurf starts. The game window's size is also the size of exported videos. Everything here is kept in tasurf.local.json.

Another map

Pick it under Game & folders and Launch, or load it in the game's console (map name). Open timeline shows the loaded map's timelines first, and Create timeline its start positions.

Reading it

Where you would go: Preview ⚡ asks the game to play your current inputs (sped up by tas_preview_timescale) and draws the exact result in white on the speed graph and in speed colours on the path, with your last real run in grey. A preview from before your latest edits is drawn dotted until the next one arrives.

The instant prediction (dashed orange, here and in the top and 3D views): from the first tick you changed, the editor plays your inputs itself, at once, with a copy of the game's movement against the map's brushes, teleports and pushes, so an edit or a drag shows where it goes before the game's preview comes back and replaces it. It can't see displacements, props, moving brushes or map logic, so it drifts the further it goes.

Speed graph: last run (light), the run before it (grey), and the prediction (dashed orange). Without the map's brushes, an estimate takes its place, from the air model starting at the run's exact state where your edit begins, and it stops at the first tick that run touched a surface, trigger or the ground: past that the model can't know the map. Timeline ▾ (by Fit) says what each thing drawn on the timeline is. The strip under the speed graph: green ground, blue ramp contact, red teleport/event.

How it works

  1. Render saves and bakes the timeline, as Run does. In the game you're moved to the spectators and watch the replay bot in first person (with the replay pitch from Look); the path drawings are hidden.
  2. The bot plays the run at normal speed (slower with motion blur, which renders many frames for each one). SVR records from tick 0 to the last tick, plus the seconds after it you set.
  3. Then you're put back on your team where you were, and the video is moved to the project's videos folder. It never replaces another file: name.mp4, then name_2.mp4, …

Before you export

The game must have been started from Setup (Launch), through SVR: TASurf checks that SVR is in it, because without SVR the game's own startmovie writes thousands of image files instead. The video's size is the game window's, set in Setup.

The settings

FormatH.264 in mp4 for small files to share; H.264 4:4:4 keeps more colour but fewer players play it; DNxHR (mov or mkv) makes large files that video editors handle best QualityH.264's CRF: lower is better and larger, 0 is lossless. Encoding: slower presets make slightly smaller files and take much longer Motion blurblends many rendered frames into each video frame, as a camera does: samples are per video frame (60 at 60 fps is 3600 frames a second of video), exposure is how much of each frame's time they cover Speed on screenSVR's speed number, of the player you watch: horizontal, 3D or vertical; its font, colours, border or shadow; where on screen (-100 to 100 across and down, 0 is the middle); how often it changes; and a delay to match the picture Camerashow the weapon or not, and the seconds of video after the last tick

SVR

Recording uses SVR (Source Video Render, github.com/crashfort/SourceDemoRender), installed separately. TASurf writes its settings as SVR's profile data/profiles/tasurf.ini before each render.
